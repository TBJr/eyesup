# Privacy

EyesUp is built so that what you type stays with you. This notice explains what the app keeps, where, and what never happens.

## What EyesUp stores

All of it is stored on your own device: in your browser's storage for the web app, or in a local database for the desktop app.

| What | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Your answers to the six starting questions and your two typing-check results | To choose your starting track and show your progress against day 1 |
| Speed and accuracy for each key and each letter pair | To decide which keys to practise and when the keyboard guide fades |
| A summary of each session and each line (speed, accuracy, which keys you missed) | To show your progress |
| Settings: layout, track, goal, display and access choices | To keep the app the way you set it |
| Texts you paste into the Library | So you can practise them again |
| A detailed keystroke log (off by default, kept 14 days if you turn it on) | Only for troubleshooting |

## What EyesUp never does

- It never sends what you type, how fast you press each key, or your saved texts to anyone, including us.
- It never reads keys outside the EyesUp window or tab. The desktop app has no background keyboard access.
- It never shows ads or shares data with advertisers.
- It needs no account, email address or name.

## Optional usage statistics

If we add usage statistics, they will be off until you switch them on, and they will contain only session-level numbers such as session length and average speed. They will never contain text or individual keystrokes.

## Your control

- **Export:** Settings → Your data → Export everything gives you a JSON file of all your EyesUp data.
- **Delete:** Settings → Your data → Delete everything removes it all from this device. This can't be undone.
- **Uninstall:** removing the desktop app or clearing site data for EyesUp in your browser also deletes everything.

## Children

EyesUp is designed for people aged 13 and over. It collects no personal details from anyone.

## Updates

The desktop app checks a public update file to see if a new version exists. That request contains no information about you or your typing.
